The user has identified a potential reviewer working in the care field. Their precise role, willingness, availability and session details remain to be confirmed. No endorsement, credentials or patient experience are inferred. Do not contact or schedule anyone without the user's direction.

## Purpose

Learn whether the workflow and explanations make sense to a caregiver. This is a small formative usability review, not a clinical trial, dietary assessment or proof of medical effectiveness. One family-connected reviewer can provide useful feedback; disclose the relationship in any account of the method and avoid calling the review independent validation. Additional participants could broaden design feedback later.

## Invitation the user may share

“I’m building CareTable, a meal-planning prototype that combines familiar tastes with practical needs. Would you be willing to try a fictional example for about 20–30 minutes and tell us what is useful or confusing? We are reviewing the software, not testing your ability or asking for patient information. It is not a medical or allergy-safety tool. You can stop at any time.”

## Before starting

- Confirm willingness to participate and the role/context they are comfortable describing. Do not assume medical qualifications from working in care.
- Explain note-taking; ask separately before recording. Public quotations, name/job disclosure and recording reuse each need explicit permission.
- Use fictional sample data. Do not enter names, diagnoses, medications or real care-recipient details.
- Explain what currently works: manual setup, planning, checks and feedback. State the unresolved voice limitation instead of asking them to diagnose a broken connection.
- Use a stable development/review build and record its revision. If it uses Local preview, say so; do not describe it as live Qloo output.

## Session tasks

1. From the landing page, explain in your own words what you think CareTable does and who it is for.
2. Open the fictional sample. Find User Mode and explain the distinction between favourites, dislikes and ingredients to avoid.
3. Add a fictional preparation preference, inspect duration/equipment, and locate the review confirmation. Observe unaided navigation before offering help.
4. Create or inspect an accurately labelled plan. Find ingredients, recipients, preparation time and checks. Ask what “cannot verify” means to them.
5. Identify where the taste connection came from and which part is CareTable's interpretation. Note any expectation that Qloo certifies safety.
6. Draft a change for a meal that takes too long. Explain what should stay unchanged. A preview can test the draft interaction; only a real saved revision tests the actual change.
7. If the build supports it, reopen the previous plan/version and describe whether the change history is clear.

## Neutral questions

- Which part fits your actual planning routine, and which part does not?
- What did you expect to happen here?
- Which wording or control made you hesitate?
- What would you need to check outside this app before using a suggestion?
- Does the explanation help you decide, or add more work?
- What is the single most important thing to improve?

Avoid “Wouldn't this save you time?” or asking the reviewer to agree that our use of Qloo is useful. If comparing two plans, keep constraints fixed, randomize presentation where practical, and ask which is more useful and why. Do not tell them which one is supposed to be better.

## After the session

Summarize observations, separate interpretation from quotation, implement agreed fixes when authorized, and record which tasks improved in a follow-up. Keep raw identifiable notes/recordings in an ignored private location. Publish only a sanitized findings summary with consent and limitations.

Example of appropriate wording **after an actual session**: “One caregiver completed a formative walkthrough using fictional data. We observed [specific finding] and changed [specific UI]. The reviewer is related to the builder; this is early usability feedback, not clinical validation.”

Do not fill those placeholders or publish any claim until the session and changes actually occur. Only approved exact quotations may appear in the README, slides or video. Never invent a testimonial or imply endorsement from the reviewer's employer.
